# Application demo video

Start by finishing this sentence: “After watching, [audience] will see how to [outcome].” Resolve the build/URL and audience from the request. If the app is unavailable, prepare a shot list or edit supplied footage. Label a prototype or illustrative mock explicitly; never substitute one as proof of a shipped feature.

## Direct the story

Choose one job. Open on the problem already visible in the product, show the decisive interaction, and end on a result a viewer can verify. Skip sign-in, setup, logos and menu tours unless they are the story. Aim for 20–45 seconds for one feature; use chapters for a longer walkthrough. For every shot write its starting state, action, visible proof, and one sentence a viewer can read without sound. Record a few seconds of clean footage either side of each action.

Use a disposable workspace or approved demo account, realistic sample names and believable amounts. Close notifications, passwords, customer records and unrelated windows before capture. Prefer recapture to blurring entire regions. Do not send emails, change real records or publish the video merely to demonstrate an action.

## Capture

For browser apps use real interactions in an isolated browser session: locate controls by role or accessible name, wait for the intended state, then record the hold. Match the viewport to the capture size; native-resolution footage is clearer than a large viewport squeezed into a tiny recording. For desktop/mobile use supplied footage or an available OS window/simulator recorder, crop to the app, and drive the same action. If screen recording permission is unavailable, say so. The editor accepts their clips too. See [capture and edit list](references/production.md).

## Edit

Start from the product's typography, color and rhythm. Keep controls and outcomes readable at half size. Move the pointer deliberately and keep it off text during holds. Use cuts for elapsed time; keep cause and effect together. If an important detail is too small, capture a closer view or add a deliberate crop for that shot. Don't use zooms or animations for decoration. Never make edits imply instant processing or a successful outcome the footage did not show.

Use the bundled renderer for a consistent 1080p cut with chapter labels, caption rail, progress line and cover frame. Its editorial labels sit outside the footage. For a different brand or aspect ratio, compose an appropriate layout and apply the same quality checks. For a walkthrough, default to local spoken narration plus captions; honor silent or supplied-audio requests. Write for the ear: one thought per shot, leave space for the action, and speak numbers and abbreviations naturally. For a conversational presenter or an A/B voice comparison, use the local neural walkthrough preset in the production reference. Preserve the previous cut for comparison. Listen for awkward pronunciation, missing words or rushed delivery. Shorten a line or give it more screen time instead of clipping it. Do not clone a person’s voice. Keep source footage and edit list editable.

## Acceptance and handoff

Watch the exported cut muted at the intended sharing size. Inspect a frame from every shot and both sides of each cut: is the target visible before the click, is its consequence obvious afterward, and can you read the caption before it changes? Check first and final frames for blank UI; give the final state a clean hold. Verify duration, resolution, playback and audio if included. A clip proving a frontend interaction does not prove a backend side effect. Deliver the actual MP4, poster and optional captions, with a clear description of what was demonstrated and any fixture or staging limitations. Do not publish without instruction.
